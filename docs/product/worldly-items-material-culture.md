# #136 Worldly Items / Material Culture — Final Product Contract

## 1. Product purpose

Worldly Items define Glantri's reusable vocabulary for ordinary material culture: the mundane objects, possessions, tools, furnishings, materials, provisions and equipment that plausibly exist in the game world.

The catalogue is intentionally independent of any particular Campaign, settlement, merchant, inventory or current application representation.

A Worldly Item family answers:

What kind of distinct material object is this, in functional terms?

It does not by itself answer:

- whether a particular Setting possesses it;
- which cultural or technological form it takes;
- how common it is;
- who owns it;
- where it is sold;
- what it costs;
- whether a particular Character can obtain it;
- whether it is portable or equippable.
  The approved base catalogue contains:

- 142 Item families
- 15 categories
  The previous ~132-family count is superseded and is not a target to preserve.

## 2. Catalogue principles

### 2.1 Function-first identity

An Item family is defined primarily by what the object is or functionally does.

Material, culture, quality, social status and technological manifestation normally do not create separate Item families.

Examples:

- Cooking pot / cauldron remains one family whether made from pottery, soapstone, bronze, iron or another appropriate material.
- Outer garment remains one family even where a culture's manifestations include cloaks, mantles, coats or analogous forms.
- Writing surface or manuscript material does not imply that paper exists universally.
- Bag or sack is not duplicated as grain sack, game bag, merchant's sack, etc.
  Conversely, objects with genuinely different functions remain distinct even where made from the same material.

A leather shoe, leather waterskin and leather pouch are therefore different Item families.

### 2.2 Avoid contextual duplicates

The catalogue should not create separate families merely because an object appears in a different context.

Examples that should normally not become separate families include:

- game bag versus bag;
- grain sack versus sack;
- field chest versus chest;
- ship's bucket versus bucket;
- mooring line versus rope;
- camp kettle versus kettle;
- military shovel versus shovel;
- iron pot versus ceramic pot.

### 2.3 Worldly Item does not mean inventory Item

A Worldly Item is:

a distinct material object, not necessarily a portable or equippable inventory object.

The catalogue may therefore contain:

- bedstead;
- loom;
- forge;
- anvil;
- kiln;
- hearth;
- large furniture;
- other fixed or semi-fixed material objects.
  Catalogue inclusion does not imply:

- portability;
- Character-inventory suitability;
- equippability;
- identical treatment of fixtures and personal possessions.

## 3. Treatment classes

Every family receives one catalogue-level treatment classification.

D — Descriptive

No bespoke gameplay mechanic is currently justified.

Ordinary physical properties may still exist.

P — Physical-profile

Generic physical characteristics are particularly important, such as:

- encumbrance;
- bulk;
- capacity;
- load;
- material;
- portability;
- transport characteristics.
  P does not imply bespoke mechanics or equippability.

M — Mechanical-review candidate

A bounded later gameplay rule may plausibly be useful.

M means only that later review is justified. It does not approve or define the mechanic.

The classification is not a quota. The former figure of roughly 47 potentially mechanical Items is superseded.

The approved base catalogue currently yields:

| Treatment | Families |
| --------- | -------- |
| D         | 60       |
| P         | 49       |
| M         | 33       |
| Total     | 142      |

## 5. Society, technology and manifestation architecture

The controlling authoring principle is:

Society Level and Arch Society influence applicability, manifestations and prevalence; they do not normally redefine the functional Item family.

The catalogue therefore separates:

family identity

from

contextual manifestation.

A manifestation answers questions such as:

- What form does this Item take here?
- What material is used?
- Can this society produce it?
- Does this technological context support it?
- Is it indigenous, adopted, imported or anomalous?
- In what broad contexts would it normally appear?
  Durable applicability dimensions may include, where meaningful:

- Society Level;
- Arch Society;
- Setting/context;
- technological prerequisites;
- material availability;
- broad economic or functional context.
  These dimensions are not required to apply equally to every Item family.

For example:

Cooking pot / cauldron remains one family while appropriate manifestations might include pottery, soapstone, bronze, iron or other forms.

The later authoring pass should test whether a society/context has credible material answers to ordinary needs including:

- clothing and weather protection;
- carrying and storage;
- food acquisition and production;
- food preparation and preservation;
- sleeping and shelter;
- lighting, fire and heat;
- hygiene and sanitation;
- craft, making and repair;
- agriculture or another subsistence model;
- travel and transport;
- trade, measurement and recordkeeping where relevant;
- leisure, music and adornment.
  A society does not need every global family.

A differing cultural manifestation does not automatically justify a new family.

## 6. Item identity and Item availability

This is a controlling architectural separation.

Item identity and Item availability are separate concerns.

Presence in the global 142-family catalogue means only:

This is a recognized ordinary-material-culture concept within Glantri.

It does not mean:

- every Setting contains it;
- every Society can make it;
- every Campaign enables it;
- every settlement possesses it;
- every merchant stocks it;
- every Character may buy or obtain it.
  The durable conceptual layering is:

Global Worldly Item Catalogue

        ↓

Contextual manifestations / applicability

        ↓

Campaign-enabled Item catalogue

        ↓

Future locality / market / merchant availability

        ↓

Actual stock / Item instances

The present contract defines the first three layers conceptually.

The fourth and fifth remain intentionally deferred.

## 7. Global catalogue

The Global Worldly Item Catalogue is the approved set of 142 functional Item families.

It is the stable generic vocabulary against which later contextual material culture is authored.

Adding cultural detail should normally add:

- applicability;
- manifestations;
- descriptive provenance;
- contextual metadata;
  rather than proliferating new functional families.

The catalogue should be reopened only where a genuinely distinct material function is discovered, not merely because another historical or cultural example exists.

## 8. Contextual manifestations and applicability

The second conceptual layer answers:

What forms of this Item family plausibly exist in this cultural, technological and Setting context?

Contextual applicability may establish that:

- a family is normal in one society;
- absent in another;
- imported in a third;
- technologically possible but unusual;
- represented through materially different manifestations;
- restricted to particular functional environments.
  No numeric occurrence probability is approved here.

No merchant stock model is approved here.

No universal Society-Level availability floor is approved here.

## 9. Campaign-enabled Item catalogue

The Campaign-enabled Item catalogue is:

the set of ordinary Item families and manifestations that may plausibly exist as part of a particular Campaign's material culture.

It should normally be derived, not manually assembled Item by Item.

The conceptual inputs are:

Campaign Setting/context

        +

approved Item applicability

        ↓

Campaign-enabled catalogue

The GM must nevertheless be able to make explicit Campaign-specific exceptions.

Examples include:

- allowing an unusual imported object;
- enabling a manifestation normally foreign to the baseline Setting;
- excluding an otherwise plausible family;
- allowing an exceptional technological or cultural survival;
- suppressing a family that is inappropriate to the Campaign premise.
  These are catalogue-level exceptions.

The GM should not be required to pre-approve every individual ordinary Item instance.

## 10. Social Band boundary

The existing Character/Profession Social Band concept must not be repurposed without a separate product decision.

It may later be relevant to questions such as:

- typical ownership;
- likely consumers;
- prestige;
- social accessibility.
  It does not presently define:

- settlement classes;
- merchant classes;
- market tiers;
- Item availability.
  Concepts such as:

- “B3 shop”;
- “B2 town”;
  must not be invented merely to solve commerce.

Future commerce needs its own locality/market/merchant context.

## 11. Future shops and markets seam

The catalogue must support, but does not yet implement, a future flow of:

Campaign-enabled catalogue

        +

locality / market context

        +

merchant type/context

        ↓

proposed or generated ordinary stock

        ↓

GM may edit exceptions

The product intent is that the GM should not normally hand-select every mundane object held by a shop or store.

Examples of future merchant contexts include:

- village general-goods seller;
- ironmonger;
- wealthy tailor;
- market stall;
- dockside chandlery;
- weaponsmith.
  The eventual system should be able to draw plausible stock from the Campaign-enabled catalogue.

This contract deliberately does not define:

- merchant-generation algorithms;
- exact stock probabilities;
- merchant wealth;
- prices;
- restocking;
- demand;
- scarcity;
- supply chains;
- economy simulation;
- purchase rules.
  The Worldly Item architecture merely preserves the necessary seam.

The classification does not constrain a future vehicle/vessel runtime model.

Inclusion in this set does not mean that a secondary Weapon Template is approved.

A later review must determine whether each object:

- naturally uses an existing Weapon Skill;
- has weapon characteristics credibly comparable to an existing purpose-built weapon;
- is sufficiently common and predictable to justify a registered secondary Weapon Template.
  That review must not introduce:

- a new Weapon Skill;
- a new fighting style;
- a new general weapon subsystem merely to accommodate the object.
  The following wider class should normally remain within a future generic improvised-object treatment rather than receive full Weapon Templates:

- scythe;
- adze;

## 14. Explicit exclusions and domain boundaries

The following remain outside the approved core Worldly Items catalogue.

Currency and coinage

Deferred to future economy/money architecture.

Medicines, remedies, poisons and specialist alchemical preparations

These require their own specialist content/rules treatment.

They should not be invented as generic mundane Items merely for catalogue completeness.

Weapons, shields and armor

Owned by the Equipment/Weapon domain.

Ordinary objects with possible secondary weapon use remain ordinary Items unless separately approved through the bounded review above.

Animals

Animals are not ordinary material objects merely because they may be:

- bought;
- transported;
- harnessed;
- traded;
- kept as possessions.
  Ships and boats

The vessels themselves await future vessel/vehicle/spatial architecture.

Their ordinary equipment remains in this catalogue.

Buildings and rooms

Buildings, rooms and larger architectural structures are not Worldly Items.

Distinct furnishings, installations and material objects within them may be.

Unique authored media/content

Physical carriers can be Items.

Their unique authored content may belong elsewhere.

For example:

- a physical book may be a Worldly Item;
- the particular text contained in it may belong to Story or Artifact architecture.
  Magical objects

Magic does not create a parallel mundane family catalogue here.

Magical identity, effects and special content remain a distinct later problem.

An ordinary object's material identity can remain recognizable where another domain later adds magical significance.

## 15. Mechanical-review boundary

M Items are not an authorization backlog for 33 bespoke mechanics.

Later mechanical review should seek small generic systems and coherent clusters, not one rule per object.

The main review clusters are:

Light, heat and fire

50–57.

Potential future concerns include illumination, ignition, duration, fuel and environmental use.

No mechanics are decided here.

Hunting, fishing and capture

99–104.

Potential future interaction with hunting/fishing/trapping competence warrants bounded review.

Shelter, riding and field travel

109–111.

Review only where travel, exposure or riding systems justify it.

Maritime equipment

116–120.

Any eventual mechanics should align with vessel/navigation architecture.

Access, leverage and lifting

121, 124–126.

These may affect what tasks are possible without becoming collections of arbitrary numerical bonuses.

Security and signaling

137–139.

Locks, restraints and signaling have obvious situational effects that may merit later formalization.

Maintenance

67.

Sharpening should receive mechanics only if a broader equipment-maintenance model warrants it.
