## How to read an entry

The short description, full description, Typical uses and Boundaries are the proposed player/GM text. Metadata appears under **Review annotations** so that context, memberships and provenance remain available for exact later transcription without becoming part of the reader explanation. Examples illustrate when a competence matters; they neither prescribe a roll nor introduce an action, benefit or prerequisite. The prose describes a capable practitioner without assigning that description a rank.

The generic Concrete Skill page remains primary. An Arch Society is an optional historical lens. The 11 recovered Arch Societies remain the working historical roster; this pass neither expands it nor closes the unresolved provenance question about the earlier recollection of 21. No fictional civilization history or new Skill × Arch Society manifestation is manufactured here.

**Context** specifies the subject or practice needed to interpret a competence: a legal system, society, milieu, language pair, body of material practice, region or similar context already recognized by Revision 2. These annotations do not introduce separate purchases, familiarity penalties, automatic universal mastery or new Specializations. Contextual detail can be supplied later without rewriting the generic competence.

**Learning Groups** represent actual shared training/practice that ordinarily trains the participating Skill. The approved 46 Pilot memberships remain intact in their own register. Future-target annotations retain or propose membership explicitly; lack of a group is an honest blank. No Specialization inherits its parent's group by default. A Profession, a Learning History and an actual Skill/Learning Group membership remain separate records of different things. No Candidate→legacy-group crosswalk is authored.

**Arch Skills** remain overlapping informational responses to recurring human problems. An entry may participate in several, and none is an acquisition, prerequisite, transfer relationship or access rule. Domains are the separate organizational headings used for this catalogue.

**Availability and mechanics remain separately authored.** Historical attestation, normal acquisition of the complete competence, structural Society Level, Arch-Society applicability, Social-Band access and Setting context are not derived from this prose or its memberships. Society Level × Social Band remains separate from Pilot DS/AB. No cost, rank threshold, relationship percentage, opposed check, transfer, combat modifier, weapon remapping, profession mechanic or learning-history rule is supplied.

**Evidence labels:** [R] recovered A1 source fact; [R2] controlling roster proposal; [PO]/[PO-FINAL] Product Owner ruling; [PO-LG2] approved Learning Groups Revision 2; [P] proposed wording or interpretation; [H] separately checked historical/craft observation. Source IDs are provenance. An inherited or provisional target ID is not a source-to-target migration instruction.

### Social Interaction

#### Deception

**ID:** `skill:deception`  
**Name:** Deception  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Social Interaction

**Short description**

Deception is the practice of making a misleading account or presentation convincing and keeping it consistent under scrutiny.

**Full description**

A capable deceiver chooses what to say, what to leave unsaid and which details will make an account believable to a particular listener. They anticipate questions, keep their claims consistent and adapt when another person notices a difficulty. A deception may involve a false explanation, a concealed intention or a carefully selected truth.

The competence concerns the construction and delivery of the misleading account. It does not provide the facts needed to impersonate every occupation, the appearance of another person or certainty about what an audience believes.

**Typical uses**

- Give a plausible explanation for an unexpected absence.
- Conceal the real purpose of a negotiation.
- Maintain a cover story when questioned about its details.

**Boundaries**

- Disguise changes apparent identity; Deception sustains misleading claims.
- Acting creates a dramatic performance and does not automatically make a lie convincing.
- Insight interprets another person's reactions; Deception does not reveal their thoughts.

**Context:** No mandatory named binding proposed; relevant practice context may inform manifestation.

#### Insight

**ID:** `skill:insight`  
**Name:** Insight  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Social Interaction

**Short description**

Insight is the interpretation of people's motives, concerns and likely intentions from their words, conduct and circumstances.

**Full description**

An insightful person listens to how an account is presented, notices changes in conduct and considers what someone stands to gain or fears losing. They compare a person's response with the situation and with what is known about that person, rather than treating a gesture or expression as proof by itself.

The result is an informed reading of another person. Fear, embarrassment, custom and simple misunderstanding can resemble concealment, so sound judgment includes recognizing when several explanations remain possible.

**Typical uses**

- Recognize that a negotiator's stated objection hides a different concern.
- Judge whether a witness is frightened, confused or deliberately evasive.
- Notice tension between a leader's public assurance and private conduct.

**Boundaries**

- Insight does not read minds or establish that a statement is false.
- Perception notices details; Insight interprets their possible human significance.
- Customs supplies knowledge of social conventions that may explain unfamiliar conduct.

#### Interrogation

**ID:** `skill:interrogation`  
**Name:** Interrogation  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Social Interaction

**Short description**

Interrogation is the planned questioning of a person to obtain a clear account and examine gaps or inconsistencies.

**Full description**

A capable questioner establishes what needs to be learned, arranges questions in a useful sequence and follows up answers without losing the central inquiry. They distinguish what a person witnessed from what they inferred or heard elsewhere, return to unclear details and compare different parts of an account.

This practice can be used with willing witnesses as well as reluctant respondents. It is concerned with directed questioning and the quality of the resulting account; pressure alone does not make an answer reliable.

**Typical uses**

- Establish the sequence of events described by a witness.
- Ask follow-up questions about a missing interval in an account.
- Separate a traveller's direct observations from repeated rumours.

**Boundaries**

- Research compares testimony with the wider body of evidence.
- Intimidation applies pressure; it does not substitute for skilled questioning or establish truth.
- Insight interprets motives and reactions rather than supplying an interview method.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** No mandatory named binding proposed; relevant practice context may inform manifestation.

**Learning Groups:** [P; PO-FINAL conditional acceptance] Full-catalogue addition: G17 — Interview and evidence practice. [P] New conditional G17 member: direct questioning, sequencing and follow-up must themselves receive instruction and feedback; gathering finished accounts is insufficient.

**Arch Skills:** [P/R2] A05 — Establishing what happened or is true; A09 — Securing agreement and commitment.

**ID status:** Proposed product ID; source migration separate.

**Provenance:** [R] Q:84 — Interrogation (qualitative name, no recovered target ID)

#### Intimidation

**ID:** `skill:intimidation`  
**Name:** Intimidation  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Social Interaction

**Short description**

Intimidation is the use of credible threat and forceful presentation to pressure another person's decision.

**Full description**

An intimidating person makes the consequences of resistance seem immediate and believable. They judge what the other person values, how much force to display and whether a threat is likely to be taken seriously. The pressure may rest on personal presence, apparent backing or a clearly stated consequence.

The competence lies in making that pressure effective in the circumstances. A threat can provoke defiance, flight or concealed resentment, and even apparent agreement may last only while the threatened consequence remains credible.

**Typical uses**

- Deter someone from interfering with a guarded entrance.
- Press a reluctant participant to abandon an obstruction.
- Make the consequences of breaking an agreement unmistakable.

**Boundaries**

- Intimidation does not guarantee obedience, loyalty or truthful answers.
- Leadership sustains shared effort; Intimidation relies on pressure.
- Deception is needed when the threat depends on a convincing false claim.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** No mandatory named binding proposed; relevant practice context may inform manifestation.

**Learning Groups:** None authored.

**Arch Skills:** [P/R2] A09 — Securing agreement and commitment; A21 — Managing appearances and misleading observers.

#### Leadership

**ID:** `skill:leadership`  
**Name:** Leadership  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Social Interaction

**Short description**

Leadership is the practice of giving people direction, maintaining their commitment and coordinating their efforts toward a shared purpose.

**Full description**

A capable leader explains what needs to be done, gives people workable responsibilities and notices when uncertainty, disagreement or exhaustion is undermining the effort. They establish confidence through judgment and conduct, adapt their approach to the people involved and keep a group acting together when circumstances change.

Leadership can develop in households, work parties, expeditions or public life. It does not depend on holding an office, and holding an office does not by itself establish the competence.

**Typical uses**

- Organize a work party after an unexpected setback.
- Restore confidence among companions facing a difficult journey.
- Resolve confusion about responsibilities during a shared undertaking.

**Boundaries**

- Military Command concerns professional command of an organized military unit.
- Battle Tactics determines what a unit should do in an engagement.
- Leadership does not supply specialist knowledge or compel agreement.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** No mandatory named binding proposed; relevant practice context may inform manifestation.

**Learning Groups:** [PO-LG2] Preserved Pilot membership: G09 — Unit coordination and command practice.

#### Oratory

**ID:** `skill:oratory`  
**Name:** Oratory  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Social Interaction

**Short description**

Oratory is the preparation and delivery of speech that explains, argues or persuades before an audience.

**Full description**

A capable speaker gives an address a clear purpose, arranges its arguments and examples, and delivers it so listeners can follow and remember it. They adjust emphasis, pace and expression to the occasion, respond to the audience and make difficult or disputed matters intelligible without losing the main point.

The competence applies to public appeals, explanations, ceremonial addresses and arguments. Its strength lies in spoken presentation; sound delivery does not make an unsupported claim true or provide knowledge of an unfamiliar subject.

**Typical uses**

- Present a proposal to an assembly.
- Explain a disputed decision to a concerned crowd.
- Deliver a commemorative speech suited to the occasion.

**Boundaries**

- Teaching includes practice, diagnosis of learning difficulties and correction beyond a successful speech.

#### Teaching

**ID:** `skill:teaching`  
**Name:** Teaching  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Social Interaction

**Short description**

Teaching is the practice of helping another person learn through explanation, demonstration, guided practice and useful correction.

**Full description**

A capable teacher identifies what a learner already understands, breaks difficult work into manageable steps and chooses examples that reveal the important point. They watch attempts closely, distinguish a misunderstanding from a lack of practice and give corrections the learner can actually use.

Teaching can be oral, practical or text-based. It may take place in a household, apprenticeship, school or informal exchange, and does not require a particular institution. The teacher's command of the subject remains separate from their ability to teach it.

**Typical uses**

- Demonstrate a craft operation and correct a learner's first attempts.
- Find why a pupil repeatedly misunderstands an explanation.
- Arrange a sequence of practice that develops a difficult technique.

**Boundaries**

- Teaching does not confer knowledge of the subject being taught.
- Oratory addresses an audience; Teaching checks and develops individual understanding.
- Knowing how to teach does not itself confer standing as a recognized teacher.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** No mandatory named binding proposed; relevant practice context may inform manifestation.

**Learning Groups:** None authored.

#### Document & Credential Forgery

**ID:** `skill:document-forgery`  
**Name:** Document & Credential Forgery  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Languages & Textual Practice

**Short description**

Document & Credential Forgery is the creation or alteration of documents and credentials intended to pass as genuine examples of a particular documentary practice.

**Full description**

A capable forger understands how the intended document is normally arranged, worded and presented, and how its details are expected to agree with one another. They reproduce a plausible appearance and account while avoiding inconsistencies that would draw attention during ordinary examination.

The work depends on the kind of record involved: an authorization, receipt or certificate each has its own conventions. Familiarity with one documentary practice does not provide command of every writing tradition, official procedure or method of making seals and materials.

**Typical uses**

- Prepare a letter of introduction supporting an assumed identity.
- Alter a document while keeping its account internally consistent.
- Judge which documentary details must agree with a claimed office or purpose.

**Boundaries**

- Written Tradition supplies reading and composition in the relevant written practice.
- Deception concerns the misleading account presented to people; this Skill concerns the document itself.
- Visual Art and specialist crafts remain distinct from documentary imitation.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** Required context: documentary, credential and authentication conventions.

**Learning Groups:** None authored.

**Arch Skills:** [P/R2] A07 — Reading and composing written language; A21 — Managing appearances and misleading observers; A22 — Passing unnoticed or bypassing controlled access.

#### Palaeography & Decipherment

**ID:** `skill:palaeography-decipherment`  
**Name:** Palaeography & Decipherment  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Languages & Textual Practice

**Short description**

Palaeography & Decipherment is the analysis of difficult, unfamiliar or historical writing to recover a defensible reading.

**Full description**

A capable practitioner compares letter forms, repeated signs, abbreviations and the arrangement of a text. They use parallel passages, known expressions and the circumstances of a document to distinguish a likely reading from an attractive guess. Damaged or unfamiliar writing often requires several possibilities to be kept open until further evidence appears.

The competence ranges from interpreting an unfamiliar hand within a known tradition to investigating a poorly understood system of writing. It does not make every script readable, and recovering signs is not the same as understanding their language.

**Typical uses**

- Expand unfamiliar abbreviations in an old account.
- Compare several copies to reconstruct a damaged passage.
- Identify repeated patterns in an otherwise unfamiliar inscription.

**Boundaries**

- A Written Tradition covers ordinary reading and composition within that tradition.
- Translation & Interpretation renders understood meaning into another language.
- Decipherment does not guarantee a solution when the available evidence is insufficient.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** Required practice context: writing system, historical hands and material corpus; no universal decipherment grant.

**Learning Groups:** None authored.

**Arch Skills:** [P/R2] A05 — Establishing what happened or is true; A07 — Reading and composing written language; A28 — Preserving and transmitting knowledge.

**ID status:** Proposed product ID; source migration separate.

**Provenance:** [R] Q:85 — Palaeography & Decipherment (qualitative name, no recovered target ID)

#### Translation & Interpretation

**ID:** `skill:translation-interpretation`  
**Name:** Translation & Interpretation  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Languages & Textual Practice

**Short description**

Translation & Interpretation is the accurate rendering of meaning between languages in speech or writing.

**Full description**

A capable translator or interpreter follows the speaker's or writer's intended meaning, recognizes ambiguities and finds an expression that serves the same purpose for another audience. They distinguish literal wording from idiom, preserve important qualifications and ask for clarification when the source leaves something uncertain.

Spoken interpreting also requires keeping track of an unfolding exchange; written translation permits closer comparison and revision. Both depend on the languages and written practices involved. The competence does not supply knowledge of an otherwise unknown language.

**Typical uses**

- Interpret a negotiation without losing conditions attached to an offer.
- Translate a letter while preserving its tone and qualifications.
- Explain why an expression has no straightforward equivalent in another language.

**Boundaries**

- Spoken Language and Written Tradition remain separately learned competences.
- Palaeography & Decipherment addresses difficult or unfamiliar writing before its meaning can be translated.
- Translation does not supply specialist legal, medical or religious knowledge merely because a text concerns it.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** Required context: language pair/direction and oral or written task. No language acquisition or transfer is implied.

**Learning Groups:** None authored.

**Arch Skills:** [P/R2] A06 — Communicating in spoken language; A07 — Reading and composing written language; A28 — Preserving and transmitting knowledge.

**ID status:** Proposed product ID; source migration separate.

#### Mathematics

**ID:** `mathematics`  
**Name:** Mathematics  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Scholarship

**Short description**

Mathematics is the use of learned methods to reason about quantities, relationships, shapes and patterns.

**Full description**

A capable mathematician turns a practical or abstract question into relationships that can be examined and worked through. They choose suitable procedures, follow the reasoning carefully and check whether an answer fits the original question. The methods and notation available depend on the mathematical tradition in which they have learned.

The competence can support calculations, geometric reasoning and the comparison of proportions or recurring patterns. It is broader than routine counting, while remaining distinct from the practical judgment needed to build, survey, navigate or manage accounts.

**Typical uses**

- Work out proportions for dividing a quantity under stated conditions.
- Examine the geometry of a proposed design.
- Find and correct an error in a chain of calculations.

**Boundaries**

- Bookkeeping maintains accounts; Mathematics supplies more general quantitative reasoning.
- Surveying measures places and dimensions in the field.
- Mathematical knowledge does not automatically include every historical or later mathematical method.

**Review annotations**

#### Meditation

**Name:** Meditation  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Scholarship

**Short description**

Meditation is a trained discipline for entering and maintaining a mental state suited to ceremonies, magical rituals and other demanding practices.

**Full description**

A capable practitioner follows a learned method of preparation, settles distracting thoughts and sustains the attention or receptiveness required by the coming activity. The method may use stillness, breathing, repeated words, imagery or another discipline appropriate to its tradition. The practitioner learns to recognize when the intended state has been reached and when it is being lost.

- It is not a general guarantee of calmness, obedience or resistance to influence.
- Mnemonics concerns deliberate memory techniques rather than preparation of mental state.

**Review annotations**

#### Mnemonics

**ID:** `skill:mnemonics`  
**Name:** Mnemonics  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Scholarship

**Short description**

Mnemonics is the deliberate use of memory techniques to organize information for later recall.

**Full description**

A capable practitioner gives information a memorable form through association, ordered imagery, rhythm, grouping or another learned method. They rehearse retrieval as well as initial learning, maintain an order among related details and use deliberate reminders to recover material that might otherwise be confused or forgotten.

The competence is especially useful when a sequence, list or extended account must be retained without continual reference to a record. It improves the handling of learned information; it does not create knowledge that was never acquired or make every recollection exact.

**Typical uses**

- Memorize the order of a long spoken address.
- Keep several messages distinct while carrying them between people.
- Build a sequence of reminders for recalling names and obligations.

**Boundaries**

- Mnemonics is trained method, not a general measure of natural memory.
- Research establishes information; Mnemonics does not establish its truth.
- Spoken Language and Written Tradition remain separate from remembering material expressed through them.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** No mandatory named binding proposed; relevant practice context may inform manifestation.

#### Philosophy

**ID:** `philosophy`  
**Name:** Philosophy  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Scholarship

**Short description**

Philosophy is the systematic examination of arguments about knowledge, conduct and fundamental ideas.

**Full description**

A capable philosopher defines the question under discussion, distinguishes competing meanings and follows the consequences of an argument. They test whether conclusions follow from their premises, identify hidden assumptions and consider objections that might require a position to be revised.

Philosophical work may be conducted through discussion, commentary or sustained writing within a learned tradition. It develops disciplined reasoning about broad questions; it does not provide all the practical knowledge of natural inquiry, religion, law or government that those questions may touch.

**Typical uses**

- Expose an unstated assumption in a disputed argument.
- Compare two accounts of what makes knowledge reliable.
- Develop a reasoned position on an obligation or dilemma.

**Boundaries**

- Theology develops systematic doctrine within a religious tradition.
- Research gathers and evaluates evidence for a particular inquiry.
- Philosophy does not absorb every practice historically called natural philosophy.

**Review annotations**

#### Research

**ID:** `skill:research`  
**Name:** Research  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Scholarship

**Short description**

Research is the deliberate gathering, comparison and evaluation of evidence to answer a question.

**Full description**

A capable researcher frames an inquiry, finds people or materials that may bear on it and keeps track of where each claim comes from. They compare independent accounts, distinguish observation from inference and revise an explanation when evidence does not fit. Useful evidence can include oral testimony, physical traces, repeated observations and written records.

Research does not inherently require literacy or an archive. Its defining work is the disciplined investigation of a question, including an honest account of what remains uncertain or cannot be established from the available material.

**Typical uses**

- Compare witnesses' accounts of a disputed event.
- Investigate how a recurring practical problem arose.
- Trace a claim through several records or oral sources to its origin.

**Boundaries**

- Perception notices evidence; Research organizes and evaluates an inquiry.
- Interrogation provides structured questioning rather than the whole investigation.
- Research does not grant reading ability or expertise in every subject investigated.

### Craft & Making

#### Alchemy

**ID:** `alchemy`  
**Name:** Alchemy  
**Competency type:** Base Skill  
**Domain:** Craft & Making

**Short description**

Alchemy is the learned investigation and transformation of materials within a particular alchemical tradition.

**Full description**

An alchemist prepares substances, observes their changes and works with the vessels, furnaces and procedures recognized by their tradition. They judge the qualities of ingredients, compare the results of repeated work and interpret those results through the tradition's account of matter and transformation.

Alchemy combines practical material work with a body of learned explanation. Its aims and methods need to be understood in their own context; the competence is neither a universal science of all substances nor inherently a form of magic.

**Typical uses**

- Examine whether a prepared substance has the qualities a procedure requires.
- Compare the results of several material preparations.
- Interpret a workshop account using the concepts of a known alchemical tradition.

**Boundaries**

- Smelting, Smithing, Pharmacy and Distilling retain their own substantial practices.
- Alchemy does not provide every later chemical method or theory.
- Any supernatural effect requires an explicit Setting or game rule.

**Review annotations**

**Context:** Required binding: alchemical tradition and learned material practice.

**Learning Groups:** None authored.

**Arch Skills:** [P/R2] X03 — Obtaining and preparing raw materials. Remedy-related alchemical traditions may later receive A03 context-specific participation; it is not universal to Alchemy.

A capable theologian compares teachings, clarifies difficult concepts and follows the arguments through which a tradition explains or disputes its doctrines. They distinguish different authorities or interpretations and relate a particular question to the wider body of learned religious thought.

The specialization extends Religion where sustained doctrinal study forms a differentiated practice. It does not require that every religion organize its teachings in the same way, and theological competence is separate from holding office or producing a supernatural effect.

**Typical uses**

- Compare competing interpretations of a doctrinal question.
- Explain how a difficult teaching fits a wider body of doctrine.
- Identify an assumption on which a theological disagreement depends.

**Boundaries**

- Religion supplies broader knowledge of teachings, practices and meanings.
- Philosophy examines wider fundamental arguments beyond a single religious tradition.
- Theology does not itself establish religious authority or magical power.
