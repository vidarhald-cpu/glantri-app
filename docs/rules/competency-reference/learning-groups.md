# Learning Groups

These 59 Learning Groups describe sustained practices in which the listed
competencies are actually taught together. A group grants no competency,
and a base-Skill membership never carries its Specializations with it.

Learning Groups have no independent Society-Level, Social-Band, or
Arch-Society availability. A contextual route must be supported through a
Profession or another approved source and must satisfy every listed
competency's own requirements.

See the [competency rules](../competency-rules.md) and [availability gaps
and exceptions](./gaps-and-exceptions.md).

The current legacy-compatibility catalogue also retains seven older choice-slot
groups in which a Character selects a stated number of Skills from a candidate
pool. Those groups are not among these 59 target Learning Groups, and their
slot grammar has not been translated into the target catalogue. See the
[choice-slot reconciliation](./profession-learning-group-skills.md#choice-slot-reconciliation).
Candidate pools are possible choices, not fixed membership.

## Patient care

**What it represents.** Learners assess patients, give immediate help and follow continuing care under an experienced practitioner.

Learners accompany a practitioner through repeated cases, discuss what they observe, carry out appropriate care and return to see how the patient changes. The teacher corrects missed signs, mistaken explanations and poorly performed care, rather than merely letting the learner watch.

This is a practice in which both immediate assistance and considered treatment are taught. Learners also receive sustained instruction in bedside care, observation and reporting. A household practitioner, healer or organized place of care can provide this instruction; the group does not depend on a modern hospital.

**Base Skills.** [First Aid](skills.md#skill-skill-first-aid); [Medicine](skills.md#skill-skill-medicine); [Nursing](skills.md#skill-nursing)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Physician](professions.md#profession-p30) (Ordinary clinical apprenticeship); [Surgeon](professions.md#profession-p31) (Integrated physician-surgeon route only)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Surgery requires deliberate operative instruction; observing operations does not supply it.
- Pharmacy requires making and dispensing preparations, rather than merely giving a prepared medicine.
- Diagnostic Medicine, Bone Setting, Obstetrics and Poison Treatment require their own specialist teaching and are not included automatically.

## Plant and remedy preparation

**What it represents.** Learners identify useful plants and turn medicinal ingredients into dependable preparations under a remedy maker.

Teaching follows ingredients from recognition and collection through preparation, preservation and dispensing. Learners compare plants and habitats, distinguish similar materials and explain why a particular ingredient or preparation is suitable. Their teacher checks both the identification and the finished remedy.

The practice includes wider plant knowledge and the practical preparation of medicines. A workshop that buys all its ingredients and teaches only preparation does not exemplify the whole group, nor does a plant walk that never develops remedies. Instruction may be oral and practical, supported by written recipes where those are used.

**Base Skills.** [Plant Lore](skills.md#skill-skill-plant-lore); [Pharmacy](skills.md#skill-skill-pharmacy)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Herbalist and Remedy Maker](professions.md#profession-p35) (Ordinary plant-remedy apprenticeship)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Medicine concerns assessing and treating illness; making a remedy does not ordinarily teach the complete clinical competence.
- Distilling and Alchemy are not included merely because a workshop uses their products.
- Poisons is a distinct body of preparation and use, rather than another name for remedy craft.

## Tracking and hunting practice

**What it represents.** Learners follow quarry, approach it unobtrusively and make hunting decisions under an experienced hunter.

Instruction takes place through repeated searches and hunts in which the learner does more than accompany the party. A teacher asks them to locate signs, follow and recover a trail, choose an approach and explain when a hunt should continue or change. Missed observations and noisy or conspicuous movements are revisited and corrected.

Observation and concealment receive their own exercises as well as field practice. This makes the group a body of taught techniques, rather than a list of everything a hunter might find useful. The quarry and methods can differ between traditions without supplying every possible weapon or trapping practice.

**Base Skills.** [Tracking](skills.md#skill-skill-tracking); [Hunting](skills.md#skill-skill-hunting); [Stealth](skills.md#skill-skill-stealth); [Perception](skills.md#skill-skill-perception)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Hunter and Trapper](professions.md#profession-p01) (Tracking-hunt route)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Weapon-family Skills require their own complete weapon instruction.
- Trapping and Trapmaking are not taught by a hunt that does not design, set or tend traps.
- Animal Lore and Survival are not established by knowing this quarry or spending time outdoors.

## Field travel and camp practice

**What it represents.** Learners plan routes, judge changing weather and maintain a workable camp through supervised travel.

An experienced traveller guides repeated journeys and camps, asking learners to choose a route, explain signs of changing conditions and provide shelter and other immediate necessities. Decisions are reviewed against the ground actually encountered and the condition of the camp and its occupants.

Route knowledge, weather judgment and practical survival are each taught throughout the experience. Merely travelling with an expedition is insufficient: a passenger or a helper who always follows instructions may learn only part of this practice. Routes and camp methods can be transmitted through demonstration, oral description and remembered landmarks.

**Base Skills.** [Survival](skills.md#skill-skill-survival); [Wayfinding](skills.md#skill-skill-wayfinding); [Weather Lore](skills.md#skill-skill-weather-lore)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Hunter and Trapper](professions.md#profession-p01) (Expedition route); [Wild Plant Gatherer](professions.md#profession-p02) (Travelling collection route); [Field and Caravan Guide](professions.md#profession-p03) (Ordinary field practice); [Driver and Teamster](professions.md#profession-p08) (Long-distance caravan service); [Courier and Messenger](professions.md#profession-p12) (Long-distance field branch); [Trader and Merchant](professions.md#profession-p13) (Overland travelling route); [Guard, Watcher and Escort](professions.md#profession-p14) (Journey-escort assignment); [Smuggler and Illicit Trader](professions.md#profession-p20) (Overland carrier branch); [Military Scout](professions.md#profession-p24) (Field-service foundation)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Athletics, Running, Climbing and Swimming require deliberate practice of their distinct movement techniques.
- Hunting, Fishing and Trapping are not included merely because food is needed.
- Dead Reckoning requires sustained instruction in estimating progress, beyond recognizing a route.

## Mounted travel and animal care

**What it represents.** Learners handle a mount on the ground and ride it through the demands of ordinary mounted travel.

Training alternates between working beside the animal and travelling on it. A teacher corrects approach, restraint, signals and responses to agitation, then works on the rider’s balance, aids, pace and judgment on changing ground. The same learner practises both sides of the partnership.

Ordinary care associated with a mount provides occasions to practise handling alongside riding. Long-term care, breeding and population management belong to the distinct Animal Husbandry competence; tending one mount does not by itself teach that entire body of work.

**Base Skills.** [Animal Handling](skills.md#skill-skill-animal-handling); [Riding](skills.md#skill-skill-riding)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Field and Caravan Guide](professions.md#profession-p03) (Mounted guide route); [Herd Keeper](professions.md#profession-p06) (Mounted herding assignment); [Courier and Messenger](professions.md#profession-p12) (Mounted branch); [Mounted Soldier and Lancer](professions.md#profession-p23) (Working mounted-service foundation); [Military Scout](professions.md#profession-p24) (Mounted-scout route); [Military Commander](professions.md#profession-p25) (Mounted-officer service route); [Envoy](professions.md#profession-p60) (Mounted travelling envoy branch)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Animal Husbandry requires sustained population-care and breeding instruction, beyond daily attention to a riding animal.
- Mounted Combat and Lance require combat training.
- Animal Training and Steppe Remount Training are specialist practices and do not follow from learning to ride.

## Working a boat

**What it represents.** Learners practise the routines and manoeuvres needed to operate a vessel under experienced crew.

Training takes place through repeated work aboard a vessel, where learners operate its equipment, respond to changing loading and conditions and coordinate ordinary manoeuvres. An experienced crew member corrects timing, handling and the learner’s interpretation of how the craft is responding.

This remains an intentionally small group. Its unity comes from the vessel’s working routines, not from the number of Skills listed. More specific apprenticeships can teach ropework or fishing alongside Seamanship, but those additions need the further instruction described on their own group pages.

**Base Skills.** [Seamanship](skills.md#skill-skill-seamanship)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Sailor and Boat Worker](professions.md#profession-p09) (General vessel-work route); [Navigator and Pilot](professions.md#profession-p10) (Working-crew entry route); [Shipmaster](professions.md#profession-p11) (Crew-service foundation); [Smuggler and Illicit Trader](professions.md#profession-p20) (Working-boat carrier branch); [Diver and Water Worker](professions.md#profession-p84) (Working-boat branch)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Swimming is not ordinarily taught by working aboard.
- Pilotage, Chart Navigation and other position-finding practices require separate navigation instruction.
- Ropework is not included merely because learners tie a few task-specific knots.
- Shipbuilding and Sailmaking concern substantial making and repair practices rather than ordinary operation.

## Passage navigation

**What it represents.** Learners estimate progress and relate a passage to landmarks, routes and local waters under a navigator.

A navigator teaches learners to keep track of movement, connect their estimated position with recognizable features and work through the local approach to a destination. Learners make their own judgments during a passage, compare them with observations and explain discrepancies before continuing.

This particular practice includes route finding and local pilotage as well as estimating progress. Its complete teaching is more than simply obeying a helmsman’s directions. Oral sailing directions and remembered coastal features can support it; charts and observations of celestial bodies are additional practices, not assumptions about all seafaring.

**Base Skills.** [Dead Reckoning](skills.md#skill-skill-dead-reckoning); [Wayfinding](skills.md#skill-skill-wayfinding); [Pilotage](skills.md#skill-skill-pilotage)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Navigator and Pilot](professions.md#profession-p10) (Passage-and-local-pilotage apprenticeship); [Shipmaster](professions.md#profession-p11) (Navigating-master branch)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Chart Navigation and Celestial Navigation require those methods to be deliberately taught.
- Seamanship concerns vessel operation and is not supplied by learning to choose a passage.
- Cartography and Surveying require making maps and reliable measurements, rather than using local directions.

## One-handed edged close-combat practice

**What it represents.** Learners fight with one-handed edged weapons while directly practising avoidance and parrying under a weapon instructor.

A teacher works through weapon control, distance and movement in paired practice, then has learners use those lessons in changing close exchanges. Avoiding an attack and meeting or redirecting it receive separate demonstrations, repetition and correction. The learner is taught all three competences, rather than being expected to acquire defensive judgment merely by holding a weapon.

This is a narrow course that can be taught by a household instructor, an independent weapon teacher or an organized civilian practice. It concentrates on armed exchanges. It does not claim to teach the whole rough-fighting, restraint or unarmed struggle practice of a watch or guard.

**Base Skills.** [1-h edged](skills.md#skill-one-handed-edged); [Dodge](skills.md#skill-dodge); [Parry](skills.md#skill-parry)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Field and Caravan Guide](professions.md#profession-p03) (Armed escort-guide assignment only); [Sailor and Boat Worker](professions.md#profession-p09) (Edged-weapon armed-crew assignment); [Shipmaster](professions.md#profession-p11) (Armed civilian command branch); [Trader and Merchant](professions.md#profession-p13) (Armed travelling route only); [Guard, Watcher and Escort](professions.md#profession-p14) (Edged-weapon route; additional to [Guard and rough-fighting drill](#group-f35) when both sets are issued); [Weapon Instructor](professions.md#profession-p82) (Compact edged-weapon instructor route)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Equipment and taught practice.** One-handed edged weapons within the unchanged 1-h edged family; no second weapon family is promised.

**Boundaries.**

- Brawling is excluded because this course does not teach sustained unarmed fighting, restraint and bodily struggles.
- Fencing is not supplied by ordinary edged-weapon lessons; [Swashbuckling fencing instruction](#group-f30) teaches the established specialization directly.
- Other weapon families, Formation Fighting and Mounted Combat require their own complete instruction.
- No separate Sword, Shield, Knife/Dagger or Spear acquisition is created.

## Unit coordination and command practice

**What it represents.** Learners decide a unit’s tactical tasks, issue workable directions and practise leading the people who must carry them out.

An experienced commander supervises exercises and service in which learners consider the situation, decide what a unit should do and turn that decision into orders, responsibilities and coordinated action. The teacher reviews both the plan and what actually happened when people tried to carry it out.

Professional command receives its own sustained instruction. Learners practise maintaining the unit’s organization and command responsibilities as well as motivating people and reasoning about tactical choices. These are related but distinct lessons; participation as an ordinary formation member does not provide the whole practice.

**Base Skills.** [Battle Tactics](skills.md#skill-tactics); [Leadership](skills.md#skill-skill-leadership); [Military Command](skills.md#skill-captaincy)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Military Commander](professions.md#profession-p25) (Command apprenticeship); [Campaign Planner](professions.md#profession-p27) (Commander-planner branch only)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Formation Fighting concerns performing effectively within a formation, not exercising command over it.
- Campaign Strategy and Military Logistics require sustained instruction beyond the tactical unit exercises described here.
- Battlefield Awareness and Combat Experience are not established by discussing plans.

## Council and mediation practice

**What it represents.** Learners listen to disputes, address a gathering and negotiate workable terms under an experienced convenor.

Learners take part in supervised discussions where they must understand the parties, explain a position publicly and explore terms that participants might accept. A teacher corrects misread reactions, poorly framed proposals and breaches of the conventions governing the encounter.

The practice includes direct teaching of social conventions, speech and negotiation. It can be learned through community deliberation and oral instruction as well as more formal councils. Resolving a dispute is the shared activity; it is not a separate base Mediation Skill and does not establish expertise in every legal system.

**Base Skills.** [Oratory](skills.md#skill-skill-oratory); [Insight](skills.md#skill-skill-insight); [Customs](skills.md#skill-skill-customs); [Bargaining](skills.md#skill-skill-bargaining)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Envoy](professions.md#profession-p60) (Ordinary representative apprenticeship); [Mediator and Community Convenor](professions.md#profession-p61) (Ordinary mediation apprenticeship); [Political Agent and Council Representative](professions.md#profession-p63) (Deliberative representative branch)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Law requires instruction in a legal system’s authorities, rules and procedures.
- Leadership is useful to a convenor but is not automatically taught to every learner in mediation practice.
- Courtly Protocol and Court Advocacy require their narrower specialist instruction.

## Records-office practice

**What it represents.** Learners organize recurring office work and maintain accounts through supervised handling of real records.

A clerk or administrator teaches learners to receive matters, preserve a usable record, follow work through its stages and keep obligations from being overlooked. Alongside this, learners enter and reconcile accounts rather than merely copying figures prepared by someone else.

The connection is repeated responsibility for both the work and its record. An office can teach these through practical apprenticeship, dictated instruction and the written conventions it uses. Access to a writing tradition and knowledge of the office’s business remain separate from the two competencies listed here.

**Base Skills.** [Administration](skills.md#skill-skill-administration); [Bookkeeping](skills.md#skill-skill-bookkeeping)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Military Quartermaster](professions.md#profession-p26) (Supply-office apprenticeship); [Clerk and Steward](professions.md#profession-p58) (Ordinary office apprenticeship); [Scribe and Copyist](professions.md#profession-p65) (Administrative-accounting scribe branch only)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Research requires inquiry and evaluation of evidence; orderly filing alone does not teach it.
- Law and Banking & Credit require their own substantive judgment.
- No named Written Tradition is included merely because records are written.
- Court Administration is not inherited automatically from Administration.

## Trading practice

**What it represents.** Learners assess goods, negotiate terms and carry transactions through under an experienced trader.

Teaching takes place through repeated buying and selling in which the learner examines goods, compares their condition and likely value, discusses terms and follows the transaction to completion. The teacher explains errors in judgment and reviews the result rather than assigning the learner only carrying or copying work.

The learner is taught the whole practical exchange: commercial decisions, negotiation and appraisal. Markets, travelling trade and fixed shops can provide this practice, but simply being present in a market does not. The goods and trading conventions can vary without making every associated financial or administrative competence a member.

**Base Skills.** [Trade](skills.md#skill-skill-trade); [Bargaining](skills.md#skill-skill-bargaining); [Appraisal](skills.md#skill-skill-appraisal)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Shipmaster](professions.md#profession-p11) (Owner-trading master branch only); [Trader and Merchant](professions.md#profession-p13) (Ordinary trade apprenticeship); [Smuggler and Illicit Trader](professions.md#profession-p20) (Trading apprenticeship)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Bookkeeping requires systematic accounts rather than remembering individual prices.
- Banking & Credit concerns obligations and financial exposure beyond ordinary buying and selling.
- Streetwise and Underworld are not supplied by commercial contact with a place or network.

## Metalworking apprenticeship

**What it represents.** Learners make and repair metal objects under a smith who corrects their working of the material.

The apprentice works through material selection, shaping, fitting and repair on repeated objects. A smith demonstrates techniques, inspects the result and has the learner correct weaknesses or repeat work until form and serviceability are reliable.

This intentionally small group describes a substantial apprenticeship even with one base Skill. Producing metal, making armor and maintaining weapons each involve further distinct work; none is taught simply because it may take place near a forge. More narrowly defined workshops below explain when another complete competence is deliberately taught alongside Smithing.

**Base Skills.** [Smithing](skills.md#skill-skill-smithing)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Smith and Weaponsmith](professions.md#profession-p36) (General forge apprenticeship)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Smelting requires managing a process that produces metal from starting material, not merely heating purchased metal.
- Armoring requires protective design and fit around a moving body.
- Weaponsmithing and Weapon Maintenance require specialist making or servicing instruction.

## Local contacts and information gathering

**What it represents.** Learners build useful local contacts and interpret what people will tell them through guided work in an urban milieu.

An experienced local guide or contact teaches learners where to seek information, how to approach people and how to keep a conversation useful without misreading its reception. Learners compare accounts from different encounters and discuss why a contact was forthcoming, guarded or misleading.

The local network and the interpretation of people are both explicitly taught. This is more than walking through a settlement or knowing its customs. It can be learned through repeated accompanied visits and oral instruction, without assuming that the milieu is illicit.

**Base Skills.** [Streetwise](skills.md#skill-skill-streetwise); [Insight](skills.md#skill-skill-insight)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Local Informant](professions.md#profession-p16) (Ordinary contact apprenticeship); [Investigator and Intelligence Agent](professions.md#profession-p17) (Settlement-inquiry branch)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Underworld requires knowledge of the relevant illicit network and its working arrangements.
- Bargaining and Deception are not established by asking questions or making introductions.
- Research requires a fuller disciplined inquiry than collecting local reports.

## Covert entry practice

**What it represents.** Learners inspect an approach, move without attracting notice and open locks through coached entry exercises.

A teacher prepares and reviews repeated entries in which learners search for relevant details, choose a quiet approach and work with familiar lock mechanisms. Afterward they examine what they overlooked, what exposed them and why a lock did or did not yield.

Observation, unobtrusive movement and manipulation of locks each receive deliberate practice. The group concerns this combined instruction, not every way of entering a place or every competence useful to someone acting covertly. Demonstrations and supervised exercises can teach it without a formal institution.

**Base Skills.** [Stealth](skills.md#skill-skill-stealth); [Perception](skills.md#skill-skill-perception); [Lockpicking](skills.md#skill-skill-lockpicking)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Thief](professions.md#profession-p18) (Burglar route)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Trapmaking requires technical knowledge of constructed traps; seeing a suspicious feature does not teach their design or bypass.
- Climbing is not included merely because some entries involve height.
- Warded-Lock Entry needs its specialist lock instruction.
- Underworld is knowledge of an illicit milieu, not an entry technique.

## Impersonation and distraction practice

**What it represents.** Learners prepare an assumed appearance and sustain a misleading account through rehearsed encounters.

Training alternates preparation with encounters in which another person questions or challenges the presentation. Learners adjust clothing, grooming and other identifying details, develop a consistent account and review the points where appearance and explanation failed to agree.

The teacher directly corrects the making of a disguise and the delivery of a deception. Encounters may involve distraction, but distracting an observer does not itself establish Sleight of Hand, Acting or another separate competence. A learner may encounter those further lessons through other instruction.

**Base Skills.** [Deception](skills.md#skill-skill-deception); [Disguise](skills.md#skill-skill-disguise)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Investigator and Intelligence Agent](professions.md#profession-p17) (Covert intelligence-service branch only); [Confidence Trickster](professions.md#profession-p19) (Ordinary impersonation apprenticeship)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Acting concerns dramatic performance and is not inferred from impersonation.
- Sleight of Hand requires deliberate manual manipulation practice.
- Customs may supply useful contextual knowledge but must be taught separately.
- Document & Credential Forgery requires making convincing documents rather than carrying them.

## Interview and evidence practice

**What it represents.** Learners conduct structured inquiries, question people and compare accounts with observable evidence under a mentor.

A mentor has learners frame questions, locate witnesses or other evidence and keep track of the origin of claims. Learners practise interviews, check what an answer actually establishes and revisit observations that do not fit. Feedback concerns the conduct of the inquiry as well as what was noticed or inferred.

Interrogation belongs in the expanded version described here because structured questioning, follow-up and evaluation of answers are expressly rehearsed. It is not inferred from any conversation. Research can proceed through oral testimony and physical observations; literacy and an archive are not inherent requirements of this practice.

**Base Skills.** [Research](skills.md#skill-skill-research); [Insight](skills.md#skill-skill-insight); [Perception](skills.md#skill-skill-perception); [Interrogation](skills.md#skill-skill-interrogation)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Investigator and Intelligence Agent](professions.md#profession-p17) (Ordinary inquiry apprenticeship)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Law requires learning a particular legal system and its procedure.
- Intimidation and Deception are not necessary constituents of structured questioning.
- Palaeography & Decipherment requires specialist instruction with writing, beyond using a readable record.
- No spoken or written competency is acquired merely because an inquiry uses it.

## Operative care practice

**What it represents.** Learners provide initial care and undertake supervised operative treatment with an experienced surgeon.

The teacher guides learners from an injured patient’s immediate needs through preparation for an intervention and the controlled work of the operation. Learners repeatedly practise initial assistance as well as operative judgment and technique, and discuss what must be watched afterward.

This is a surgical apprenticeship that actually teaches both listed competences. Being a bystander to an operation, handing over instruments or providing only later bedside care does not cover the whole practice. Its methods depend on the operative tradition and circumstances without turning Surgery into a specialization of Medicine.

**Base Skills.** [Surgery](skills.md#skill-skill-surgery); [First Aid](skills.md#skill-skill-first-aid)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Surgeon](professions.md#profession-p31) (Ordinary operative apprenticeship)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Boundaries.**

- Medicine requires a broader clinical practice than the operative work described here.
- Nursing requires sustained bedside-care teaching, beyond brief assistance around an operation.
- Battlefield Surgery and other medical specializations require their own narrower instruction.

## Field surveying and map production

**What it represents.** Learners measure ground and turn their checked observations into usable maps under a field surveyor and map maker.

## Military crossbow and formation practice

**What it represents.** Learners develop crossbow handling and shooting while practising coordinated movement and their place in a crossbow formation.

An instructor corrects preparation, alignment, handling and shot judgment, then has learners carry out their work amid the movement and space needs of other crossbow users. Learners rehearse signals, changes of position and recovery of their place after the arrangement is disturbed.

The course teaches complete crossbow practice and collective participation, not merely a loading sequence. It is a ranged component of a soldier’s training. Close fighting is taught elsewhere, and operating a complete emplaced engine is a distinct task even where its weapon belongs to the existing Crossbow family.

**Base Skills.** [Crossbow](skills.md#skill-crossbow); [Formation Fighting](skills.md#skill-formation-fighting)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Soldier](professions.md#profession-p22) (Military crossbow track, combined with [One-handed arms and formation drill](#group-f36)); [Siege Engine Crew Specialist](professions.md#profession-p29) (Existing Crossbow-family engine route)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Equipment and taught practice.** The familiar crossbows taught by the unit, within the unchanged Crossbow family; coordinated movement and formation participation. No large-engine emplacement or melee weapon is promised.

**Boundaries.**

- A complete military crossbow background also needs a separately taught complete close-combat package.
- Siege Engine Operation is not included: emplacement and complete engine-crew work are not described by this course.
- The group does not narrow Crossbow’s frozen family or remove its existing large-weapon mappings.
- Bow, Firearms, Weapon Maintenance and Locksmithing are not supplied by crossbow lessons.

## Military firearms and formation practice

**What it represents.** Learners handle and shoot handheld firearms while practising coordinated movement and their place in a formed firing unit.

An instructor repeatedly corrects preparation, handling, alignment and the firing practices appropriate to the unit’s firearms. Collective exercises teach the learner to maintain working space, respond to signals, change position with companions and recover the group’s arrangement after disruption.

This is a ranged component, not a soldier’s whole fighting education. Its lessons concern the issued firearms and collective participation. The group does not describe fighting with a gun as a club, a bayonet, a sword or a pike without the separate instruction for those actual uses.

**Base Skills.** [Firearms](skills.md#skill-skill-firearms); [Formation Fighting](skills.md#skill-formation-fighting)

**Explicit Specializations.** None.

**Professions providing access.** [Soldier](professions.md#profession-p22) (Military firearms track, combined with [One-handed arms and formation drill](#group-f36))

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Equipment and taught practice.** The handheld firearms actually issued for the described unit practice, within the one Firearms Skill; coordinated movement and formation participation. No cannon or bayonet method is promised.

**Boundaries.**

- Close-combat defence, Brawling and melee weapons belong to the accompanying complete close-combat course.
- Firearms is not split into pistol, rifle or musket base Skills.
- No bayonet classification, gun-as-club mapping, cannon practice or new combat action is authored.
- Weapon Maintenance, Smithing and Alchemy are not supplied by ordinary firing routines.

## Longbow hunting apprenticeship

**What it represents.** Learners hunt with a longbow while receiving complete archery instruction and sustained correction of specialist longbow technique.

A hunting teacher alternates archery lessons with supervised hunts. Learners practise the broader bow competence, then receive further repeated correction in the longbow’s particular handling demands. In the field they choose an approach, judge when to attempt a shot and review whether continuing the hunt is sensible.

The same learner is taught the course of the hunt as well as the shooting. This is one specific longbow hunting apprenticeship, not every civilian archery lesson. It teaches neither formation participation nor human close-combat fighting, and it does not imply that ordinary Bow instruction always includes a specialization.

**Base Skills.** [Bow](skills.md#skill-bow); [Hunting](skills.md#skill-skill-hunting)

**Explicit Specializations.** [Longbow](skills.md#specialization-longbow) (parent: [Bow](skills.md#skill-bow))

**Professions providing access.** [Hunter and Trapper](professions.md#profession-p01) (Longbow-hunting tradition only)

**Supported availability.** General/unbound. Every Profession-linked
contextual opportunity remains unresolved as a bound acquisition route;
family presence alone does not establish this complete practice, its
equipment, its teachers, or admission.

**Equipment and taught practice.** Longbow practice within the approved Bow / Longbow parent-specialization relationship, used in a full hunting apprenticeship. No close-fighting weapon is taught.

**Boundaries.**

- Tracking, Stealth and Perception are not added merely because they are useful to a hunt; this page does not teach those entire competences.
- Dodge, Parry and Brawling are not included because this is not a human close-combat curriculum.
- Formation Fighting is not taught by hunting with another person.
- A military archer does not gain Hunting or Longbow merely from belonging to [Military bow and formation practice](#group-f38).
- This is not a new bow type, new specialization or weapon-access rule.
