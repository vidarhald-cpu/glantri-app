# Societies and availability

Society determines whether a Profession or training route can be sustained and
whether a character can ordinarily enter it. Society does not determine a
character's competence. A capable person may come from any supported route,
and a prestigious office does not grant the Skills needed to perform it.

This page presents the current availability framework. It does not invent the
unfinished local institutions, traditions, or Setting bindings listed in the
[availability gaps and exceptions](./gaps-and-exceptions.md).

## Society Level

Society Level describes the structural support ordinarily needed to reproduce
an occupation or training practice. It is a capacity, not a ranking of human
worth, intelligence, morality, or culture.

| Level | Structural support                                                                          |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Community and household transmission can sustain recurring practices.                       |
| 2     | Sustained specialist practice can reproduce itself.                                         |
| 3     | Organized technical or institutional continuity can be maintained.                          |
| 4     | Institutions or regions can coordinate continuing specialist work.                          |
| 5     | Several specialist teaching traditions can interact and reproduce.                          |
| 6     | Distributed specialist institutions and practices can be reproduced across a wider society. |

A Profession supported at a lower level does not disappear at a higher level.
Higher levels make additional specialization possible, but do not guarantee
that every possible Profession, institution, or training route is present.

A family-level range is only the broad occupational envelope. Each track,
Learning Group, direct competency, material requirement, teacher, and admission
route must still be supported in the actual society.

## Social Band

Social Band describes ordinary access to entry and instruction. It does not
describe eventual wealth, expertise, rank, office, or prestige.

| Band                   | Game-facing meaning                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Common Folk            | Household, communal, laboring, service, ordinary recruit, and other broadly accessible routes where local support exists. |
| Trades and Guilds      | Established craft, commercial, corporate, or specialist working routes.                                                   |
| Established Households | Landed, prosperous, professional, institutional, or patron-supported routes.                                              |
| Court and Elite        | Courtly, senior, privileged, restricted, or unusually well-supported routes.                                              |

These are not universal social-class titles. A society may organize access in
different ways, and a route may span several bands.

Availability is not automatically inherited upward. A Common-Folk route is not
necessarily open to every higher-band character, and an elite household may
employ people who entered through ordinary service. Citizenship, kinship,
religion, sex, geography, patronage, guild membership, secrecy, and local
custom may impose separate conditions.

## Arch Societies

An Arch Society is a historical lens used to ask how a competence, occupation,
or institution may appear in a kind of society. It is not a Setting, a Campaign,
or a universal civilization ladder.

The structural references above support the current Profession availability
ledger. They do not establish a universal chronological sequence, a measure of
civilizational value, or final Setting definitions.

Two Arch Societies at the same Society Level may differ in occupations,
institutions, literacy, law, religion, materials, military practice, navigation,
and teaching traditions. Arch-Society applicability is therefore authored
separately from Society Level.

## Availability states

| State          | Meaning                                                                                                                    |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Available      | The Profession or route is ordinarily compatible with and supported by the Arch Society, subject to its stated conditions. |
| Conditional    | The route depends on additional context that must be supplied.                                                             |
| Unresolved     | Current evidence does not establish the answer.                                                                            |
| Not applicable | Positive evidence or the defining structure of the Arch Society supports exclusion.                                        |

Absence of evidence normally produces Unresolved, not Not applicable. This is
especially important for oral, household, illicit, informal, and socially
marginal occupations.

## Reading Profession availability

Read availability from broadest to narrowest:

1. **Profession family:** can the occupation reproduce itself here?
2. **Track or route:** does this form of the occupation exist here?
3. **Training opportunity:** are the complete practice, teacher, institution,
   equipment, material, and admission conditions present?
4. **Competency context:** is any required language, law, religion, geography,
   written tradition, or other named binding supplied?

A broad Available result never supplies a narrower answer. The
[Profession reference](./professions.md) therefore shows family envelopes and
keeps the individual opportunities unresolved until the actual context is
bound.

## Context bindings

Some competencies can be described generically but used only after a real
context is named. Common binding families include:

- spoken language and Written Tradition;
- law, jurisdiction, court, and political institution;
- local custom, polity, and diplomatic relationship;
- illicit or clandestine milieu;
- waters, routes, vessel traditions, and geographic hazards;
- materials, technologies, workshops, and production traditions;
- military equipment, formation, mount, and weapon traditions;
- alchemical, religious, theological, divinatory, astronomical, and
  astrological traditions;
- heraldic, courtly, musical, artistic, literary, dramatic, and gaming
  traditions.

A binding explains which real practice the competency concerns. It does not
grant the competency, invent a universal version, or waive the requirements of
a Skill, Specialization, or Learning Group.

## Current limits

The general competency reference is intentionally unbound. It supports the
approved Skills, Learning Groups, Professions, and family-level availability
envelopes without applying unfinished contextual filters.

Final contextual use still requires:

- completion of family, track, and individual-opportunity availability where
  the current evidence is unresolved;
- actual language, writing, law, religion, geography, material, military, and
  institutional bindings where required; and
- a supported Common-Folk communal or civilian protection-and-fighting route
  for the Forager / ritual-tribal lens.

Until those facts exist, the reference reports the gap instead of choosing a
Setting fact by similarity.
