# Competency rules

Glantri describes what a character can learn through three connected kinds of
game content: **Skills**, **Learning Groups**, and **Professions**. Skills are
the competencies a character possesses. Learning Groups describe practices in
which several competencies are taught together. Professions describe
occupational routes through which a character may gain access to Learning
Groups or to individual competencies.

## Skills

A **Skill** is a concrete, learnable competence. It identifies a recurring set
of tasks a character can attempt, not a job title, a social position, or a
subject heading.

A **base Skill** stands on its own. A **Specialization** is a narrower
competence with an explicit parent. A Specialization remains a separate thing
to learn. Knowing its parent does not automatically grant it, and knowing one
Specialization does not grant its siblings.

Some Specializations have another Specialization as their immediate parent.
That chain remains intact. For example, Falconry is learned through Animal
Training, whose parent is Animal Handling. A training route that mentions
Falconry does not flatten or bypass that chain.

A Skill keeps one identity wherever it appears. If several Learning Groups or
Professions provide routes to the same Skill, they lead to the same
competence. They do not create duplicate versions, extra levels, or stacked
benefits.

Subject headings and cross-references help readers find Skills. They do not
grant competence or create relationships. Similar names, shared materials, or
membership in the same field likewise create no unstated transfer,
prerequisite, or availability rule.

## Learning Groups

A **Learning Group** is a recognizable shared practice in which its listed
competencies are ordinarily taught together. It may represent household
instruction, an apprenticeship, a workshop, a school, a crew, a unit, an
office, a campaign, or another sustained setting for teaching and correction.

Membership means that the complete listed competence is taught as part of that
practice. Mere exposure is not enough. Handling a few ropes does not establish
Ropework, seeing crops does not establish Plant Lore, and carrying a weapon
does not establish its fighting Skill.

Learning Groups may overlap. The same Skill can belong to several groups when
each group describes a different credible way of learning it. Overlap does not
duplicate the Skill or multiply a purchase.

Base-Skill membership and Specialization membership are explicit and separate:

- a base Skill's membership does not include its Specializations;
- a Specialization's membership does not include its parent or siblings;
- explicit Specialization membership provides a training route but does not
  waive the Specialization's normal immediate-parent eligibility.

A Skill may deliberately belong to no Learning Group. Such a Skill remains a
valid competency and may still be learned through an approved direct route.
No one-Skill group is invented merely to make the lists symmetrical.

Membership is access, not acquisition. It grants no Skill, level, experience,
point, discount, or other automatic benefit.

## Professions

A **Profession** is an occupational identity and a route to plausible
instruction. It is not a character class and does not replace the underlying
competencies.

A Profession may provide:

- access to a complete Learning Group; or
- direct access to a particular Skill or Specialization when no suitable
  shared-practice group describes the instruction.

Direct opportunities are first-class routes. They are not converted into
artificial Learning Groups. Several routes may reach the same competence, but
they still resolve to one competency identity.

A Profession **family** is the durable occupational identity. An explicit
**track** is a complete, named form of that occupation. Where tracks exist,
their identity and package remain distinct. A named training route is not a
track unless the Profession reference explicitly identifies it as one.

Family-common opportunities apply to every explicit track only when they are
authored as common. Track-specific opportunities belong only to that track.
Nothing else is inherited by similarity.

Profession packages may be unequal. A broad profession may offer several
Learning Groups; a narrow craft may offer only direct instruction in its
defining competence. Neither is expanded merely to match the other.

Selecting a Profession grants no competency, level, experience, office,
authority, equipment, wealth, Social Band, or extra resource pool. It exposes
the approved opportunities that the character may acquire under the ordinary
Character-generation rules.

Two Profession-specific alternatives apply:

- Sailor and Boat Worker offers either Rigged-vessel deck apprenticeship or
  Working a boat for that purchase; the fuller rigged-vessel route replaces
  the smaller route on that path.
- Guard, Watcher and Escort offers either Swashbuckling fencing instruction or
  One-handed edged close-combat practice for that purchase. Guard and
  rough-fighting drill may still be combined with One-handed edged
  close-combat practice when both are genuinely taught.

These alternatives do not create tracks, prohibit independent purchases, or
apply to other Professions.

## Availability

Availability answers whether a Profession or training route can be sustained
and entered in a particular context. It is separate from competence: evidence
that an occupation exists does not prove that every associated route is
available, and access to a route does not grant what it teaches.

### Society Level

**Society Level** describes the minimum structural support ordinarily needed
to reproduce a Profession, track, or training route. It is not a measure of a
society's worth, a character's ability, or a simple chronological ladder.

A practice supported at a lower Society Level does not disappear at a higher
one. Higher levels make more specialization possible, but they do not place
every theoretically possible Profession in every society.

### Social Band

**Social Band** describes ordinary access to entering or learning a route. It
does not describe eventual prestige, skill, wealth, rank, or office.

Availability need not be symmetrical across bands. A lower-band route is not
automatically inherited by every higher band, and service in an elite
household does not automatically make entry elite. Citizenship, kinship,
religion, sex, geography, patronage, guild membership, secrecy, and local
custom may create independent conditions.

Common Folk must remain a viable occupational environment. Skilled household,
craft, protective, and ordinary soldiering routes may begin there when the
local institutions and teaching support them.

### Arch Society and local context

An **Arch Society** is a historical lens for asking how a competence or
occupation may appear in a kind of society. It is independent of Society
Level. Two societies with the same structural capacity may have different
institutions, materials, military practices, laws, religions, navigation
traditions, and routes to instruction.

Arch-Society availability uses four states:

- **Available**: ordinarily compatible with and supported by that Arch
  Society, subject to the stated conditions;
- **Conditional**: possible only when additional context is supplied;
- **Unresolved**: current evidence does not settle the question;
- **Not applicable**: positive evidence or the society's defining structure
  supports exclusion.

Missing evidence normally means Unresolved, not Not applicable.

Some competencies require a named local binding: for example an actual
language, Written Tradition, legal system, religion, body of water, route,
material tradition, weapon tradition, institution, or illicit milieu. A
generic Profession can be valid before such a binding is chosen. Until then,
the content is usable in an unbound reference but is not a certified
Setting-specific route.

Availability is checked at three separate levels:

1. Does the Profession family exist here?
2. Does the particular track or training route exist here?
3. Are the complete Learning Group or competency, its materials, teachers,
   equipment, and admission conditions supported here?

A positive answer at a broader level never supplies the narrower answers.

## Character generation

Character generation uses this structure as access, not as a source of free
competence.

Before spending resources, a player can inspect the complete membership of a
Learning Group and the complete opportunity package of a Profession or track.
An available Profession route permits the corresponding purchase under the
ordinary point and cost rules. Flexible funds may pay for an already accessible
group, but do not create access by themselves.

The existing group price covers only the group's base-Skill members. An
explicit Specialization in a Learning Group is access-only and is purchased
separately: 4 points initially and 2 points for each increase, subject to its
normal immediate-parent eligibility. Multiple valid routes never multiply the
purchase, discount, or resulting competency.

The general Character-generation catalogue is intentionally **unbound**. It
does not apply unfinished Society-Level, Social-Band, Arch-Society, or Setting
filters. A campaign that activates contextual availability must use completed
bindings and preserve every unresolved case rather than borrowing old
availability formulas or guessing from occupational names.

## Related concepts

- A **Learning History** records how a particular character learned something.
  It does not substitute for authored Learning Group or Profession membership.
- An **Arch Skill** describes a recurring human problem or capability area for
  explanation and coverage review. It is not purchasable and grants no
  competence or transfer.
- Historical Profession and Skill records preserve provenance. A historical
  relationship becomes a current rule only when it is separately present in
  the approved references.

The rule throughout is simple: use explicit relationships, keep one identity
for each competency, and leave unsupported context unresolved.
